# Operational Reasoning Engine (ORE)

## Purpose

The Operational Reasoning Engine (ORE) is the decision-making core of Project Sentinel.

It transforms validated operational evidence into explainable operational conclusions.

Unlike conventional AI systems that generate answers directly, ORE follows a structured reasoning process similar to the analytical approach used by experienced enterprise engineers.

Its objective is to reduce uncertainty while improving the quality and consistency of operational decisions.

---

# Philosophy

Every recommendation shall be justified.

Sentinel never jumps directly from alerts to conclusions.

Instead, it follows structured operational reasoning.

Evidence

↓

Context

↓

Relationships

↓

Business Impact

↓

Operational Risk

↓

Reasoning

↓

Recommendation

↓

Engineer Decision

---

# Vision

Sentinel shall reason like the best enterprise operations engineer.

It shall understand the enterprise before recommending actions.

---

# Core Objectives

The Operational Reasoning Engine shall:

- Analyse available evidence.
- Detect operational relationships.
- Evaluate multiple possibilities.
- Eliminate unlikely scenarios.
- Estimate operational confidence.
- Recommend the next logical action.
- Explain every conclusion.

---

# Inputs

The Operational Reasoning Engine receives information from:

- Incident Understanding Engine
- Evidence Intelligence Engine
- Enterprise Memory Fabric
- Enterprise Intelligence Model
- Operational Policies

# Reasoning Principles

## Principle 1

Never assume.

Reason only from available evidence.

---

## Principle 2

Support multiple hypotheses.

The first explanation may not be the correct explanation.

---

## Principle 3

Eliminate before concluding.

Remove impossible or low-probability scenarios first.

---

## Principle 4

Business impact always influences reasoning priority.

---

## Principle 5

Every recommendation must remain explainable.

---

# Operational Reasoning Cycle

Every investigation follows the same reasoning sequence.

## Stage 1

Understand the Incident

Questions include:

- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Who detected it?
- Which service is affected?

---

## Stage 2

Understand the Enterprise Context

Questions include:

- Which business service?
- Which location?
- Which infrastructure?
- Which teams?
- Which dependencies?

---

## Stage 3

Build Possible Scenarios

Examples

- ISP Failure
- Hardware Failure
- Configuration Error
- Power Failure
- Software Defect
- Capacity Issue
- Human Error
- Planned Maintenance

Multiple possibilities may exist simultaneously.

---

## Stage 4

Evaluate Evidence

Each scenario is compared against available evidence.

Evidence may strengthen or weaken each possibility.

---

## Stage 5

Calculate Operational Confidence

Every possible explanation receives a confidence score.

Confidence continuously changes as additional evidence becomes available.

---

## Stage 6

Recommend Next Actions

Recommendations are prioritised according to:

- Operational value
- Business impact
- Evidence quality
- Risk
- Investigation effort

---

# Explainable Intelligence

Every recommendation shall answer:

Why?

Which evidence supports it?

Which assumptions remain?

Which evidence is still missing?

What alternative explanations exist?

---

# Human Decision Support

ORE supports engineers.

---

# Learning

Every completed incident improves future reasoning.

Successful investigations strengthen reasoning models.

Incorrect assumptions become learning opportunities.

The Operational Reasoning Engine continuously evolves.
